AHA/ACC Guidelines for ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI) Management
Reference: 2013 ACCF/AHA Guideline, 2022 Update

DIAGNOSIS CRITERIA:
- ST-elevation >= 1mm in 2 or more contiguous leads on 12-lead ECG
- New or presumed new left bundle branch block (LBBB)
- Elevated cardiac biomarkers (troponin I or T above 99th percentile)
- Clinical presentation: chest pain, diaphoresis, dyspnea, radiation to arm/jaw

IMMEDIATE MANAGEMENT (Class I Recommendations):
- Activate cardiac catheterization lab immediately
- Target Door-to-Balloon (D2B) time: < 90 minutes
- Aspirin 162-325mg chewed immediately (unless contraindicated)
- P2Y12 inhibitor loading dose (Ticagrelor 180mg or Clopidogrel 600mg)
- Anticoagulation: Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) weight-based protocol
- Supplemental oxygen ONLY if SpO2 < 90%
- Morphine for pain if needed (use with caution — may increase mortality)
- Nitroglycerin sublingual (contraindicated if systolic BP < 90 or right ventricular MI)

PRIMARY PCI (Percutaneous Coronary Intervention):
- Preferred reperfusion strategy when available within 120 minutes of first medical contact
- Radial access preferred over femoral (lower bleeding risk)
- Drug-eluting stent preferred over bare metal stent
- Complete revascularization may be considered for multivessel disease

FIBRINOLYTIC THERAPY:
- Only if PCI not available within 120 minutes
- Door-to-needle time target: < 30 minutes
- Contraindications: active bleeding, recent surgery, stroke history, aortic dissection

POST-MI MANAGEMENT:
- Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for minimum 12 months
- Beta-blocker within 24 hours (if no contraindications)
- ACE inhibitor within 24 hours (especially if EF < 40%)
- High-intensity statin therapy
- Cardiac rehabilitation referral
- Echocardiogram to assess LV function

RISK STRATIFICATION:
- TIMI Risk Score for STEMI (0-14 points)
- GRACE Score for in-hospital mortality
- Killip Classification for heart failure severity
- Factors: age, heart rate, systolic BP, Killip class, anterior MI, time to treatment

SPECIAL POPULATIONS:
- Elderly (>75): Higher bleeding risk, adjust anticoagulation
- Diabetic patients: Higher mortality, aggressive glucose control
- Renal insufficiency: Contrast-induced nephropathy risk, hydration protocol
- Women: May present atypically, same treatment standards apply
